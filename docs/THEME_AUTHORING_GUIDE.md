# Theme Authoring Guide

Status: **Frozen — a consolidated checklist for authoring Themes 4–10.** This
document adds no new rules. It synthesizes what Themes 1–3 (the platform's
three reference implementations) actually established as frozen, drawing
from `docs/THEME_EXPERIENCE_TEMPLATE.md`, `docs/VOCABULARY_PHILOSOPHY.md`,
`docs/VOCABULARY_ARCHITECTURE.md`, `docs/READING_WRITING_ARCHITECTURE.md`,
`docs/WRITING_PHILOSOPHY.md`, `docs/EDITORIAL_WORKFLOW.md`,
`docs/EDITORIAL_GUIDELINES.md`, and the frozen manuscripts themselves
(`docs/THEME_01_ADVANCED_READING_BLUEPRINT.md`, `docs/THEME_02_MANUSCRIPT.md`,
`docs/THEME_03_MANUSCRIPT.md`). Where this document and a source document
ever disagree, the source document governs — this is a distillation, not a
replacement.

Use this as the pre-flight and post-flight checklist for every theme from
Theme 4 onward. It does not replace the editorial workflow's gated phases
(§ Quality Checklist below) — it tells you what those phases are checking
*for*.

---

## 1. Educational Philosophy

- The goal is not to teach English for its own sake — English is the medium
  through which students prepare academically, linguistically, and
  personally for university.
- Every activity has exactly one educational purpose. Reading develops
  understanding; Writing develops expression and revision; AI Talk develops
  conversational fluency. None should quietly do another's job.
- AI supports thinking; it never replaces it. Every AI surface offers
  options the student can accept or reject, never silent replacements.
- Support should visibly decrease as level increases — nothing is a
  permanent crutch.

## 2. Essential Question

Every theme resolves to **one sharp, genuinely unresolved question** — not a
topic, not a moral, a question with real intellectual tension and no obvious
answer. Pattern, verbatim from the frozen manuscripts:

- Theme 1: *"Is personality mainly the product of nature or nurture?"*
- Theme 2: *"Is being a university student something that simply happens to
  you — or something you have to actively become?"*
- Theme 3: *"Is designing a future mainly about choosing a destination, or
  about understanding yourself?"*

Foundation and Advanced share the same broad theme but **Advanced asks its
own version of the question, or a deepened one** — it does not restate
Foundation's resolution in harder vocabulary. If a candidate Essential
Question already has an obvious answer, it isn't sharp enough yet.

## 3. Passage Structure

- **Two independent, complete manuscripts per theme** — Foundation and
  Advanced. Advanced is never an appended paragraph or a parallel story; it
  is its own standalone piece that happens to share a theme.
- **Length**: Foundation 180–220 words; Advanced 500–650 words.
- **Paragraph count**: Foundation ~4 paragraphs; Advanced ~4–5. One idea
  per paragraph — no paragraph should try to carry two.
- **Every target word (Foundation and Advanced) appears naturally in its
  own passage**, including natural inflections. If a word doesn't fit
  naturally, that's a vocabulary-selection problem, not a license to force
  it in — flag it upstream rather than contorting the sentence.
- Vocabulary is cumulative across levels (Advanced = Foundation +
  additional words); the passage itself is not.

## 4. Article Tone

**All three frozen reference passages (Foundation and Advanced, all three
themes) are direct expository/reflective essays — none has a named
protagonist or tells anyone's individual story.** They open a question,
reason through it in the second person or in general terms ("you," "we,"
"many people," "students"), and arrive at a considered position. This is a
deliberate departure from the older narrative-anecdote style still used in
the unmigrated Themes 4–10 legacy content ("I read about Mio/Haruto/Sora...").

This is worth being explicit about because `docs/EDITORIAL_WORKFLOW.md` still
uses protagonist/story language in its own Phase 0–1 wording (it was written
while an earlier, protagonist-driven Theme 3 draft was still in play — see
that manuscript's own revision history). The *judgment* those phases apply —
causal completeness, an earned conclusion, genuine tension, "would this
survive on its own merits" — still fully applies to essay-argument
structure; only the literal word "protagonist" doesn't map cleanly onto the
current form. Treat the workflow's evaluation questions as applying to the
argument's development, not literally to a character.

Register: reads like something a thoughtful person would actually write —
never a vocabulary-delivery vehicle with stilted "target word" placement.

## 5. Opening Style

Every frozen passage opens the same way: **state the common, intuitive
belief first — sometimes literally as a question — then complicate or
reframe it.** Never opens with scene-setting, a narrative hook, or "Once
upon a time" framing.

- Theme 1 F: *"Will personality change over time? Some people think... Others
  believe..."*
- Theme 2 F: *"What does it really mean to be a university student? At
  first, the answer seems simple... But very quickly..."*
- Theme 3 F: *"Many people think that designing a future begins by deciding
  what they want to become. This idea seems reasonable because... However,
  there may be another way..."*

The same shape holds at Advanced, just with a more developed opening move
(Theme 1 Advanced opens with a concrete observation before naming the
nature/nurture question explicitly).

## 6. Paragraph Organization

A consistent four-beat shape across every frozen Foundation passage:

1. **Pose the question/tension** (the common belief, or the passage's own
   opening question).
2. **Complicate or deepen it** — introduce the "however"/reframing move.
3. **Develop it** — reasoning, examples, or evidence.
4. **Resolve into the takeaway** — earned by the previous paragraphs, not
   bolted on as an unrelated moral.

Advanced passages extend this shape with more paragraphs of genuine
development (competing evidence, a synthesis paragraph) rather than simply
restating the same four beats in harder language.

## 7. Foundation vs. Advanced Relationship

Both levels share identical architecture (same four activities, same
Reading/Writing structure) — nothing about *which components exist*
changes. What changes:

| | Foundation | Advanced |
|---|---|---|
| Guidance | High, open by default | Available, collapsed by default |
| Language | Simple, concrete, personal | Authentic, richer, CEFR B1–B2 |
| Comprehension | 2 literal + 1 vocab-in-context | 1 inference/synthesis + 1 competing-evidence + 1 vocab-in-context |
| Writing task | Answer a question, 2–3 sentences | Explain an opinion, 80–120 words |
| Thinking | Recall, description | Inference, evaluation, independent stance |

**Foundation does not mean easy.** It means the indispensable concepts for
the theme, chosen for conceptual necessity — not the simplest words that
happen to fit. Target learners' receptive vocabulary already exceeds their
productive vocabulary, so Foundation may include words meant to be
recognized, not necessarily produced fluently.

**Advanced is a different angle, not a harder version.** Each frozen Advanced
passage reframes the theme through a genuinely different lens rather than
restating Foundation's conclusion: Theme 1 reframes as nature-vs-nurture,
Theme 2 as externally-imposed vs. self-generated structure, Theme 3 as
destination vs. self-understanding. If an Advanced draft reads like
Foundation with swapped-in vocabulary, it has not yet found its angle.

## 8. Vocabulary Philosophy

- One coherent curriculum across all ten themes, not ten independent lists.
  Consider the whole curriculum before finalizing any single theme's set.
- Targets: ~10 new Foundation words/theme (tolerance 8–12), ~8 new Advanced
  words/theme (tolerance 6–10). Cumulative: ~100 Foundation, ~180 Advanced
  by Theme 10.
- Every candidate word must pass four questions: Is it essential (not
  merely thematically decorative)? Does it naturally belong to this theme?
  Has it already appeared elsewhere (prefer reinforcing over a
  near-synonym)? Will students actually use it in Reading/Writing/AI Talk?
- **Spiral reinforcement, active from Theme 2 on**: important words should
  recur in later themes, ideally at least twice, 2–3 themes apart. When
  reinforced, a word appears at the same level or higher than its original
  introduction — never downgraded. Prefer reinforcing an existing word over
  introducing a near-synonym.
- Organize into meaningful lexical sets (by meaning), chosen per theme —
  never one flat list, never a universal category template reused across
  themes.
- Vocabulary needed to *analyze or argue about* the theme (not merely
  understand its core concepts) belongs at Advanced, regardless of how
  abstract-sounding it is.
- The goal is retention across the whole curriculum, not word count.

## 9. Writing Philosophy

- Reading asks "can the student understand?" Writing asks "can the student
  express meaningful ideas?" — Writing is not a comprehension test.
- The passage is a starting point, not the subject to summarize. Focus
  shifts from the passage to the student's own opinions, experiences, and
  reflections.
- Foundation tasks: opinions, experiences, reasons, examples — a coherent
  paragraph in simple but effective English.
- Advanced tasks: reflect, apply the reading to one's own life, compare
  ideas, explain reasons, organize multiple related ideas — richer
  expression, not a harder academic question. Advanced Writing must not
  become another Reading comprehension exercise.
- Sentence count is the only completion requirement; word count (when a
  theme states a target) is always advisory, never a gate.
- Word bank/vocabulary is offered as a resource, not a checklist — students
  are never expected to use every word.

## 10. AI Talk Philosophy

- Primary objective: conversational English fluency and comfort, not
  tutoring students about the passage. The theme is the conversation's
  reason to begin, not its learning objective.
- The AI is a friendly conversation partner — not an examiner, not a
  reading tutor, not continuously checking comprehension.
- Models natural spoken-English discourse markers ("That's interesting,"
  "Really?", "What happened next?") through repeated natural exposure, not
  explicit teaching.
- No repeated empty praise ("Excellent!", "Perfect!") unless genuinely
  warranted — should feel like talking with a supportive person, not an
  oral exam.
- Once started, the conversation follows the student's own interests,
  staying only loosely tied to the theme.
- **The opener introduces the topic, not the conclusion.** It references
  something concrete the student already engaged with, but must not spoil
  or state the passage's own resolved argument/conclusion before the
  student has said anything — pose an open question or situation, not a
  resolved claim. (This was the exact defect found and fixed in an earlier
  Theme 3 opener, and independently found unfixed in Theme 2 Advanced.)
- Foundation: shorter turns, more conversational support, more modeled
  expressions. Advanced: longer, more spontaneous, deeper opinions, richer
  natural back-and-forth — depth of conversation, not just harder English.
- Success criterion: *"I just had a real conversation in English,"* not
  *"I answered another set of questions."*

## 11. Things We Intentionally Avoid

- **No third level.** Two levels only (Foundation/Advanced) — never
  reintroduce Standard/Challenge language or thinking in new content.
- **No silent architecture/UI changes.** From Theme 4 on, reuse Theme 1's
  architecture and Theme 2/3's content shape exactly. If a theme seems to
  need something different, stop and explain before changing anything —
  "this would be an improvement" is never sufficient justification alone.
- **No forced vocabulary.** A word never earns its place just because it
  fits the topic or hits a quota — every word must pass the four selection
  questions in §8.
- **No completion gates on soft signals.** Completion never depends on
  whether an AI call was made, succeeded, or was read; word count never
  gates Writing completion.
- **No narrative protagonist in the essay-style passages** — see §4. (Not a
  hard rule against story-based content elsewhere on the platform, but the
  three frozen reference passages establish essay form as the current
  standard to match.)
- **No summarizing the reading in the AI Talk opener** — see §10.
- **No forcing vocabulary into a story that doesn't want it.** If a passage
  feels contorted to fit a required word list, that's a signal the
  vocabulary selection may not suit this theme's strongest angle — escalate
  it as a vocabulary-curriculum question, not a sentence-level patch.
- **No patching a broken foundation to avoid rework.** If the core
  concept/passage is weak, replacing it is better than repeatedly repairing
  it (`docs/EDITORIAL_WORKFLOW.md`'s core philosophy, learned from Theme 3).
- **No editing the manuscript to match a derived component.** The
  manuscript is the one-way source of truth; if Notice Language, Mission
  Check, Writing, or AI Talk reveals a possible problem in the manuscript,
  report it — never silently patch around it, and never edit the manuscript
  to accommodate a downstream component.
- **No ambiguous pronouns, no unstated logical bridges, no non-standard
  collocations in vocabulary examples** (see `docs/EDITORIAL_GUIDELINES.md`).
- **No AI Talk claims beyond what the passage established** — never state
  as fact something the passage only implies or leaves open.

## 12. Quality Checklist Before a Manuscript Is Considered Acceptable

Follow `docs/EDITORIAL_WORKFLOW.md`'s six gated phases in order — never
skip ahead, never proceed "for now" with a plan to fix a bigger issue later:

1. **Concept Review** — read the passage's argument as a one-paragraph
   pitch. Does it genuinely express the theme's Essential Question, or just
   sit near the topic? Is this the strongest available way to teach it?
2. **Content Integrity Review** — read the full argument as a complete
   piece of reasoning. Is the causal chain complete? Does it actually
   engage the Essential Question, or only gesture at it? Would this survive
   as a short reflective essay on its own merits? Is the takeaway earned by
   what came before it, not bolted on?
3. **Pedagogical Architecture Review** — does target vocabulary appear
   naturally (not forced)? Do Notice Language, Mission Check, Writing
   prompt, and AI Talk opener each genuinely derive from *this* passage,
   not generic scaffolding? Does the whole chain (Reading → Writing → AI
   Talk) flow as one integrated experience, not four independently-correct
   but disconnected pieces?
4. **Line Edit** — every sentence checked against
   `docs/EDITORIAL_GUIDELINES.md`'s principles (pronoun clarity, no
   unnecessary inference, authentic collocations, target word survives its
   own example, AI Talk stays within what the passage established, writing
   prompts are answerable by every student, AI Help answers the actual
   task, cross-material consistency).
5. **Cross-Activity Consistency Recheck** — re-read the whole theme
   end-to-end hunting for damage introduced by Phase 4's edits (a target
   word accidentally dropped, a paraphrase now disagreeing with the
   passage).
6. **Fresh-Eyes Final Read** — read once more as a first-time student
   would. The bar is *"are we proud to publish this,"* not merely *"no
   remaining flagged issues."*

A theme is not ready for Theme 5+ to build on until Phase 6 formally signs
off — no earlier phase, however clean, constitutes approval on its own.
