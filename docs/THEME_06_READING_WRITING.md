# Theme 6 Reading & Writing Plan
## Peace and War — Foundation

Status: **Approved — design complete, not yet implemented in code.**
First theme built under the revised Foundation philosophy (indispensable
concepts, not easy vocabulary).

---

## 1. Reading Passage (182 words)

**Paragraph 1**
Yuna had heard her grandmother mention the war before, but she had
never really asked about it. One quiet afternoon, while they were
drinking tea together, she finally did: "Grandma, what was it like?"
Her grandmother paused for a long moment, looking out the window,
before she began to speak.

**Paragraph 2**
When she was young, her grandmother said, her family had to leave
their home because of the fighting nearby. They became refugees for
almost a year, moving from place to place with only what they could
carry, never quite feeling safe. As a civilian, she was never a
soldier, but the war still changed everything about her daily life,
from what she ate to where she slept.

**Paragraph 3**
Her grandmother said that the war created many victims, even among
people who never fought. When the fighting finally ended and the two
sides signed a peace treaty, her family could return home. Now, she
wanted Yuna to know this piece of family history — not as something
sad to carry, but as a legacy of surviving something very hard and
choosing peace afterward.

**Takeaway Sentence**: "The war created many victims, even among
people who never fought."

## 2. Vocabulary coverage

All eight frozen Foundation words present: peace (P3 ×2), war (P1,
P2), victim (P3), refugee (P2), civilian (P2), treaty (P3), history
(P3), legacy (P3).

**Spiral note**: none reinforce an earlier theme — history and legacy
are first introduced here, to be reinforced in Theme 9.

## 3. Notice Language (4 activities)

- Find today's target vocabulary.
- Notice useful expressions/collocations ("paused for a long moment,"
  "never quite feeling safe").
- Notice paragraph organization — a frame narrative: a question (P1)
  leads to a personal account (P2), which leads to reflection (P3).
- Consider the author's purpose — telling this through a
  grandmother's own words rather than a general historical
  description, personalizing history.

## 4. Comprehension (3 tasks)

**Task 1 — Choose the Answer**
*Question*: According to the passage, why did Yuna's grandmother and
her family have to leave their home?
- A. They wanted to visit a different city for a vacation.
- B. Fighting nearby made it unsafe for them to stay. ✅
- C. Yuna's grandmother decided to become a soldier.

**Task 2 — Supporting Evidence**
*Prompt*: Which paragraph explains what daily life was like for
Yuna's grandmother during the war?
- Paragraph 1 (the conversation begins, no details yet)
- Paragraph 2 (her family became refugees, and her daily life is
  described) ✅
- Paragraph 3 (the war ends, and she reflects on its meaning)

**Task 3 — Vocabulary in Context**
*Question*: In "a legacy of surviving something very hard," what does
"legacy" mean?
- A. Money or property left to someone after a person dies.
- B. Something meaningful passed down from the past that still
  matters now. ✅
- C. A type of official government document.

## 5. Writing Task

**Prompt**: "Yuna learned something important by asking her
grandmother about the past. Is there a story or lesson from an older
family member that you know, or would like to ask about? What is it,
or what would you like to know?"

**Length**: 2–3 sentences (minimum 2).

**Optional sentence starters**:
- "My grandmother/grandfather once told me..."
- "I would like to ask about..."
- "One important lesson in my family is..."

**Useful Vocabulary for This Theme**: peace, war, victim, refugee,
civilian, treaty, history, legacy. These are the words students have
learned this theme. Use whichever ones naturally help you express your
ideas — you are not expected to use every word. The goal is natural
communication, not vocabulary coverage.

**Why this fits Foundation**: describes a family story or a question
the student would like to ask — not international conflict, politics,
or historical responsibility.

**Connection to the reading**: structural, not topical — students
experience the same kind of learning Yuna experienced, not asked to
write about war itself.

**Connection to AI Talk**: previews an opener — "Yuna learned
something important by talking with her grandmother. Is there someone
older in your family you would like to talk to? What would you ask?"
