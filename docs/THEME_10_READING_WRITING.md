## 1. Reading Passage (185 words)

**Paragraph 1**
Yuto pulled a cold can of juice from the fridge on a hot afternoon,
and within a minute, the outside was covered in tiny drops of water.
The can was sealed — nothing could be leaking out. He became curious:
where was all that water coming from? He turned the can over, checking
for a crack, but found nothing.

**Paragraph 2**
At first, he guessed it might be a tiny hole in the can — a simple
hypothesis, but one he could test. He grabbed a dry glass, filled it
with ice, and set it beside the can as a small experiment. Within
minutes, the glass was just as wet as the can, even though it had
never touched any liquid outside.

**Paragraph 3**
His hypothesis was wrong — the water wasn't leaking from inside at
all. Through careful observation, Yuto realized the water was coming
from the warm air itself: when warm air touched something cold, it
cooled down and turned into tiny drops of liquid. It was a small,
everyday phenomenon, but knowing the science behind it made an
ordinary summer afternoon feel a little more interesting.

**Takeaway Sentence**: "Science begins with curiosity about ordinary
things."

## 3. Notice Language (4 activities)

- Find today's target vocabulary.
- Notice useful expressions/collocations ("took over," "set it
  beside," "cooled down").
- Notice paragraph organization — the passage models the scientific
  method concretely: an observed puzzle (P1) → a hypothesis and a
  test (P2) → a corrected conclusion through observation (P3).
- Consider the author's purpose — showing a wrong first hypothesis
  being revised through testing, modeling that being wrong is a
  normal, productive part of scientific thinking rather than a
  failure.

## 4. Comprehension (3 tasks)

**Task 1 — Choose the Answer**
*Question*: According to the passage, what did Yuto discover about the
water on the outside of the can?
- A. The can had a tiny hole that was leaking juice.
- B. The water came from the warm air, not from inside the can. ✅
- C. Someone had spilled water on the can earlier.

**Task 2 — Supporting Evidence**
*Prompt*: Which paragraph shows Yuto testing his first hypothesis with
an experiment?
- Paragraph 1 (Yuto notices the water and wonders where it came from)
- Paragraph 2 (Yuto sets up a small experiment with a dry glass and
  ice) ✅
- Paragraph 3 (Yuto discovers his hypothesis was wrong)

**Task 3 — Vocabulary in Context**
*Question*: In "a simple hypothesis, but one he could test," what does
"hypothesis" mean?
- A. A fact that has already been proven true.
- B. A first guess or possible explanation that can be tested. ✅
- C. A tool used to measure temperature.

*Design note*: targets a genuine, realistic misunderstanding — students
often assume "hypothesis" means an already-proven fact or a formal
law, rather than a testable first guess. Domain-specific contextual
understanding, consistent with the approach established since Theme
7.

## 5. Writing Task

**Prompt**: "Yuto's curiosity about a simple wet can led him to think
and test like a scientist. Is there something in your everyday life
that makes you wonder 'why does this happen?' What is it? If nothing
comes to mind right now, describe one everyday thing you would like to
understand better."

**Length**: 2–3 sentences (minimum 2).

**Optional sentence starters**:
- "I've always wondered why..."
- "One everyday thing I'm curious about is..."
- "I would like to understand..."

**Useful Vocabulary for This Theme**: science, observation, hypothesis,
phenomenon, experiment, curiosity. These are the words students have
learned this theme. Use whichever ones naturally help you express your
ideas — you are not expected to use every word. The goal is natural
communication, not vocabulary coverage.

**Why this fits Foundation**: describes a personal curiosity or
question — no explanation of scientific mechanism or justification
required, matching the "Answer" tier.

**Connection to the reading**: mirrors Yuto's own arc directly —
noticing something ordinary, feeling curious, and wondering why —
inviting the student to name their own version of that curiosity.

**Connection to AI Talk**: previews an opener — "Yuto got curious about
something ordinary — a wet can of juice — and investigated it like a
scientist. Is there something in your everyday life you're curious
about?"
