# Theme 4 Reading & Writing Plan
## Living Together — Foundation

Status: **Approved — design complete, not yet implemented in code.**

---

## 1. Reading Passage (197 words)

**Paragraph 1**
Mio and her new roommate did not agree on almost anything. Mio liked a
quiet room for studying, while her roommate liked to invite friends
over and talk late into the night. By the end of their first week
together, they had already argued about it more than once, and
neither one knew how to bring it up calmly.

**Paragraph 2**
One evening, Mio decided to talk honestly with her roommate instead of
staying upset. She explained that she needed quiet time to focus, and
her roommate explained that she felt lonely without company. Neither
of them was wrong — they simply had different needs. They agreed to
try something new: quiet hours in the evening, and a shared night out
once a week.

**Paragraph 3**
It was not always easy, and they still disagreed sometimes about small
things. But they learned to respect each other's differences, like
good neighbors sharing the same small space. Slowly, they began to
trust each other more, and their relationship grew closer. By the end
of the semester, Mio realized that getting along with someone did not
mean always agreeing — it meant being kind, showing respect, and
trying to see things from the other person's side.

**Takeaway Sentence**: "Getting along with someone doesn't mean always
agreeing — it means being kind enough to listen."

## 2. Vocabulary coverage

All ten frozen Foundation words present: relationship (P3), trust
(P3), close (P3, as "closer"), neighbor (P3), kind (P3), respect (P3
×2), agree (P2, P3), disagree (P3), get along (P3), share (P2, P3).

**Spiral note**: kind and share are first introductions here,
fulfilling reservations made in Theme 1.

**Design principle applied**: "understanding" was deliberately avoided
in the passage and in Comprehension Task 3, since it is the finalized
Theme 4 **Standard**-level word — natural synonyms ("trying to see
things from the other person's side"; "more emotionally connected to
each other") were used instead, per the vocabulary-collision principle
established during this theme's review.

## 3. Notice Language (4 activities)

- Find today's target vocabulary.
- Notice useful expressions/collocations ("bring it up calmly," "quiet
  hours," "getting along with someone").
- Identify reference words — "each other's" in "respect each other's
  differences."
- Consider the author's tone/purpose — both roommates' needs are
  presented as equally valid ("Neither of them was wrong"), modeling
  fair-minded conflict-handling.

## 4. Comprehension (3 tasks)

**Task 1 — Choose the Answer**
*Question*: According to the passage, how did Mio and her roommate
finally solve their problem?
- A. They stopped spending time together so they would not argue.
- B. They talked honestly about their different needs and found a
  compromise. ✅
- C. Mio's roommate agreed to always do things Mio's way.

**Task 2 — Supporting Evidence**
*Prompt*: Which paragraph shows the specific moment when Mio and her
roommate found a solution together?
- Paragraph 1 (the conflict, no solution yet)
- Paragraph 2 (they talk honestly and agree on quiet hours and a
  shared night out) ✅
- Paragraph 3 (the long-term reflection on their relationship)

**Task 3 — Vocabulary in Context**
*Question*: In "their relationship grew closer," what does "closer"
mean?
- A. Physically nearer in distance, like standing close together.
- B. More emotionally connected to each other. ✅
- C. Having less free time available.

## 5. Writing Task

**Prompt**: "Think about someone you get along well with. What helps
your relationship? Or, if you disagree with someone sometimes, what
helps you solve the problem?"

**Length**: 2–3 sentences (minimum 2).

**Optional sentence starters**:
- "I get along well with..."
- "Sometimes we disagree, but..."
- "I think it is important to..."

**Useful Vocabulary for This Theme**: relationship, trust, close,
neighbor, kind, respect, agree, disagree, get along, share. These are
the words students have learned this theme. Use whichever ones
naturally help you express your ideas — you are not expected to use
every word. The goal is natural communication, not vocabulary
coverage.

**Why this fits Foundation**: describes a personal experience or
simple opinion — no analysis, justification, comparison, or
argumentation required.

**Connection to the reading**: uses Mio's experience as a bridge to
the student's own relationships, not a summary task.

**Connection to AI Talk**: previews an opener — "Mio learned that
getting along with someone doesn't mean always agreeing. Has that ever
happened to you?"
