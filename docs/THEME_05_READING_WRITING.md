## 1. Reading Passage (190 words)

**Paragraph 1**
"Drinking cold water right after a meal causes cancer." Haruto saw the
message in a group chat one evening, shared by a friend with three
exclamation points. It sounded serious, and it was already getting
dozens of replies. Part of him wanted to believe it immediately and
share it with his family before checking whether it was true.

**Paragraph 2**
Instead of sharing it right away, Haruto decided to check where the
claim came from. He searched for the source and found that no real
doctor or hospital had ever said this. The message was not true — it
was just something someone had made up and passed along. Haruto felt a
little embarrassed that he had almost believed it so quickly.

**Paragraph 3**
After that, Haruto started asking one simple question before believing
anything online: is this a fact, or just someone's opinion dressed up
to look real? He learned to doubt messages that sounded too dramatic,
and to trust sources that were honest about where their information
came from. Not everything online is fake, but it is always worth a
second look before you believe it and pass it on to someone else.

**Takeaway Sentence**: "Not everything online is fake, but it is
always worth a second look before you believe it."

## 2. Vocabulary coverage

All ten frozen Foundation words present: true (P1, P2), fake (P3),
believe (P1, P2, P3), doubt (P3), honest (P3), source (P2), check (P2,
P3), trust (P3), fact (P3), opinion (P3).

**Spiral reinforcement**: trust reinforces Theme 4 at the same level,
applied to sources and information rather than people.

## 3. Notice Language (4 activities)

- Find today's target vocabulary.
- Find recycled vocabulary from previous themes (trust, from Theme 4).
- Notice useful expressions/collocations ("dressed up to look real,"
  "worth a second look," "passed along").
- Consider the author's purpose — the passage opens with the false
  claim itself, in quotation marks, before any context, mirroring how
  misinformation actually reaches people.

## 4. Comprehension (3 tasks)

**Task 1 — Choose the Answer**
*Question*: According to the passage, what did Haruto do before
deciding whether to trust the message?
- A. He asked all his friends if they had also seen it.
- B. He checked where the claim came from and looked for its source.
  ✅
- C. He shared it with his family right away, just to be safe.

**Task 2 — Supporting Evidence**
*Prompt*: Which paragraph shows Haruto actually checking the
information before believing it?
- Paragraph 1 (Haruto sees the message and almost believes it)
- Paragraph 2 (Haruto checks the source and learns it isn't true) ✅
- Paragraph 3 (Haruto's reflection on what he learned)

**Task 3 — Vocabulary in Context**
*Question*: In "Haruto decided to check where the claim came from,"
what does "check" mean?
- A. To make a small mark like a ✓ on paper.
- B. To look into something carefully to see if it is true. ✅
- C. To ask a restaurant for the bill.

## 5. Writing Task

**Prompt**: "Haruto almost believed a message that was not true. Have
you ever seen information online that turned out to be false? What
happened? If not, what would you do to check if something is true
before you believe it?"

**Length**: 2–3 sentences (minimum 2).

**Optional sentence starters**:
- "One time, I saw..."
- "I checked by..."
- "Before I believe something, I..."

**Useful Vocabulary for This Theme**: true, fake, believe, doubt,
honest, source, check, trust, fact, opinion. These are the words
students have learned this theme. Use whichever ones naturally help
you express your ideas — you are not expected to use every word. The
goal is natural communication, not vocabulary coverage.

**Why this fits Foundation**: describes a personal experience or a
simple checking habit — no media-literacy analysis or debate required.

**Connection to the reading**: begins with Haruto's experience, then
connects to the student's own.

**Connection to AI Talk**: previews an opener — "Haruto learned to
check information before believing it. What about you? How do you
decide whether something online is true?"
