# Theme 8 Reading & Writing Plan
## Living with AI — Foundation

Status: **Approved — design complete, not yet implemented in code.**

---

## 1. Reading Passage (195 words)

**Paragraph 1**
Kenta typed his essay question into an AI tool and watched as a full
paragraph appeared in seconds. It was so convenient — no searching, no
staring at a blank page — that he almost forgot to think about whether
it was actually good. His goal had been to finish quickly, and this
felt like exactly what he needed.

**Paragraph 2**
But when his teacher asked him to explain his own opinion out loud the
next day, Kenta realized he could not remember why the essay said what
it said. He had not checked whether the ideas were even true — he had
simply copied them. This kind of technology could save time, but
automation could not do his thinking for him. Taking responsibility
for his own work suddenly felt much harder than it should have.

**Paragraph 3**
After that, Kenta decided to use AI differently. He still asked it for
ideas sometimes, curious about how its algorithm could suggest angles
he had not considered, but now he always checked those ideas carefully
and wrote the final essay in his own words. AI could help him start,
he realized, but the thinking — and the responsibility for it — still
had to be his.

**Takeaway Sentence**: "AI could help him start, but the thinking —
and the responsibility for it — still had to be his."

## 2. Vocabulary coverage

All five frozen Foundation words present: AI (P1, P3 ×2), convenience
(P1, as "convenient"), technology (P2), automation (P2), algorithm
(P3).

**Natural connections to earlier themes** (narrative echoes, not
formal Theme 8 target words, so not tracked as spiral reinforcement):
goal (Themes 1/3), responsibility (Theme 2, appearing twice — the
passage's real turning point), checked and true (Theme 5's exact
fact-checking spirit, applied here to AI-generated content instead of
a shared message), angles (echoes Theme 6's "perspective" without
using the literal word, which is reserved as Theme 6's Challenge-level
term).

## 3. Notice Language (4 activities)

- Find today's target vocabulary.
- Find recycled vocabulary from previous themes (goal, responsibility,
  checked, true) — genuinely applicable here, unlike most earlier
  Foundation passages.
- Notice useful expressions/collocations ("staring at a blank page,"
  "do his thinking for him").
- Consider the author's tone/purpose — the passage shows both AI's
  usefulness (suggesting angles) and its limits (can't take
  responsibility), avoiding either extreme.

## 4. Comprehension (3 tasks)

**Task 1 — Choose the Answer**
*Question*: According to the passage, why did Kenta struggle when his
teacher asked him to explain his opinion?
- A. He had forgotten to bring his notebook to class.
- B. He had copied the AI's ideas without checking or truly
  understanding them. ✅
- C. His teacher had asked a question about a different topic.

**Task 2 — Supporting Evidence**
*Prompt*: Which paragraph shows Kenta changing how he uses AI after
his experience in class?
- Paragraph 1 (Kenta uses AI without thinking twice)
- Paragraph 2 (Kenta realizes the problem)
- Paragraph 3 (Kenta decides to check ideas and write in his own
  words) ✅

**Task 3 — Vocabulary in Context**
*Question*: In "curious about how its algorithm could suggest
angles," what does "algorithm" mean?
- A. A robot that can talk and think like a human.
- B. A set of steps or rules a computer program follows to do
  something. ✅
- C. A company that makes computers and phones.

*Design note*: Task 3 targets a genuine, realistic misunderstanding —
students (and many adults) often conflate "algorithm" with "robot" or
an AI company itself, rather than the specific process/rules sense
used here. Domain-specific contextual understanding, consistent with
the approach established in Theme 7.

## 5. Writing Task

**Prompt**: "Kenta learned that AI could help him start, but the
thinking had to be his. If you use AI to help with schoolwork, what is
one thing you would do to make sure your ideas are really your own? If
you don't use AI, describe one way you make sure your ideas are
original when you write."

**Length**: 2–3 sentences (minimum 2).

**Optional sentence starters**:
- "If I use AI, I would..."
- "One way to make sure my thinking is my own is..."
- "I always try to..."

**Useful Vocabulary for This Theme**: AI, technology, automation,
convenience, algorithm. These are the words students have learned this
theme. Use whichever ones naturally help you express your ideas — you
are not expected to use every word. The goal is natural communication,
not vocabulary coverage.

**Why this fits Foundation**: describes a personal practice or
intention — not a debate about AI's merits. The prompt asks "what
would you do," not "is AI good or bad."

**Connection to the reading**: mirrors Kenta's own resolution directly
— checking ideas and keeping the final thinking his own — inviting the
student to name their own version of that practice.

**Connection to AI Talk**: previews an opener — "Kenta learned that AI
could help him start, but the thinking had to be his. How do you make
sure your own thinking stays part of the process?"
