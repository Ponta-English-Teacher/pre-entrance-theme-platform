# Pre-Entrance Theme Platform
## Curriculum Map (Version 1)

This document defines the official curriculum for Version 1.

Future conversations and development should always refer to this document rather than inventing or replacing themes.

The purpose of this document is to keep the curriculum stable while the learning materials are developed.

Current official themes are:

1. Knowing Myself
2. What Does It Mean to Be a University Student?
3. Designing My Future
4. Living Together
5. Facts or Fake?
6. Peace and War
7. Caring for Our Planet
8. Living with AI
9. Learning from History
10. Science in Everyday Life

---

## 1. Knowing Myself

### Purpose
Help students explore their own identity, values, and personal growth in English.

### Essential Questions
- Who am I, and how do I describe myself?
- How do I grow and change over time?
- What experiences have shaped who I am becoming?

### Natural Language Functions
Describing oneself; expressing opinions; narrating personal experience and change.

### Expected Vocabulary Areas
identity, personality, values, confidence, growth, character

### Reading & Writing Focus
A short narrative about a student's personal growth through a small act of courage; writing a personal opinion grounded in the student's own experience.

### AI Talk Focus
A natural conversation continuing the reading's theme — inviting the student to share their own experience of change or confidence.

### Notes
Already implemented — the platform's original pilot theme, with a complete Foundation-level Reading & Writing lesson and AI Talk conversation live today.

## 2. What Does It Mean to Be a University Student?

### Purpose
Prepare students to think about the transition into university life and the kind of student they want to become.

### Essential Questions
- What does it mean to be a university student, beyond just attending classes?
- What will be different from high school?
- What kind of student do I want to be?

### Natural Language Functions
Comparing high school and university life; expressing expectations and concerns; talking about independence and responsibility.

### Expected Vocabulary Areas
independence, responsibility, academic life, campus, expectation, adjustment

### Reading & Writing Focus
A passage on the transition into university life; writing about the student's own expectations or concerns.

### AI Talk Focus
A conversation imagining university life and discussing hopes or worries about the transition.

### Notes
`Theme` entry exists in the codebase (`university-student`); no reading or vocabulary content yet.

## 3. Designing My Future

goal, career, dream, ambition, choice, opportunity

### AI Talk Focus

## 5. Facts or Fake?

### Purpose
Build critical thinking and media literacy skills in English.

### Essential Questions
- How do I know what's true?
- Why do people believe false information?
- How should I evaluate what I read online?

### Natural Language Functions
Expressing doubt and certainty; evaluating evidence; giving reasons and counter-reasons.

### Expected Vocabulary Areas
information, evidence, source, reliable, misleading, opinion vs. fact

### Reading & Writing Focus
A passage on misinformation or media literacy; writing an evaluation of a claim or source.

### AI Talk Focus
A conversation discussing a claim or news story and reasoning together about whether to trust it.

## 6. Peace and War

### Purpose
Introduce students to thinking about conflict, peace, and history's lessons through English.

### Essential Questions
- Why do conflicts happen?
- What does peace require?
- What can an individual do?

### Natural Language Functions
Expressing opinions on sensitive topics respectfully; discussing cause and effect; reasoning about difficult issues.

### Expected Vocabulary Areas
peace, conflict, cooperation, history, understanding, community

### Reading & Writing Focus
A passage on a peace-related story or historical event; writing a reflective opinion.

### AI Talk Focus
A carefully guided conversation about peace and understanding between people.

### Notes
Sensitive theme — will need particular care in system-prompt design. AI Talk's current safety guardrails instruct the assistant to gently redirect *away* from violence; this theme will need those guardrails tuned so historical/educational discussion of conflict remains possible without being treated as an off-limits topic. `Theme` entry exists in the codebase (`peace-and-war`); no reading or vocabulary content yet.
