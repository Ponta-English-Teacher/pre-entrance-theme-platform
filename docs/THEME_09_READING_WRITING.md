# Theme 9 Reading & Writing Plan
## Learning from History — Foundation

Status: **Approved — design complete, not yet implemented in code.**

---

## 1. Reading Passage (184 words)

**Paragraph 1**
Rin found an old photograph tucked inside her grandmother's desk
drawer — a young woman standing proudly in front of a small shop that
no longer existed. "Who is this?" Rin asked. Her grandmother smiled
and explained that the woman was Rin's own ancestor, who had run that
little shop during a very different era, long before either of them
was born.

**Paragraph 2**
The shop had closed suddenly after one event: a fire that swept
through the neighborhood one winter night. That single event changed
the whole family's history — without the shop, Rin's ancestors had to
find new work and move to a different part of the city. One cause,
her grandmother said, had led to so many effects, some of which were
still shaping the family even now.

**Paragraph 3**
Rin looked at the photograph again, seeing it differently now. It was
not just an old picture — it was a small piece of her family's
legacy, one event from long ago still quietly shaping her life today.
History, she realized, was not only something written in books; it
was also her own story, waiting to be noticed.

**Takeaway Sentence**: "History was not only something written in
books; it was also her own story, waiting to be noticed."

## 2. Vocabulary coverage

All seven frozen Foundation words present: ancestor (P1, P2 as
"ancestors"), era (P1), event (P2 ×2, P3), history (P2, P3), cause
(P2), effect (P2, as "effects"), legacy (P3).

**Spiral note**: history and legacy reinforce Theme 6 at the same
level, now becoming this theme's own central focus.

## 3. Notice Language (4 activities)

- Find today's target vocabulary.
- Find recycled vocabulary from previous themes (history, legacy —
  Theme 6).
- Notice useful expressions/collocations ("tucked inside," "swept
  through the neighborhood," "waiting to be noticed").
- Consider the author's structure — a frame narrative built around a
  discovered object (an old photograph), where a present-day question
  opens into a past event and closes with personal reflection.

## 4. Comprehension (3 tasks)

**Task 1 — Choose the Answer**
*Question*: According to the passage, why did Rin's ancestor's shop
close?
- A. Rin's ancestor decided to retire early.
- B. A fire swept through the neighborhood one winter night. ✅
- C. The city asked the family to move to a new location.

**Task 2 — Supporting Evidence**
*Prompt*: Which paragraph explains what happened to the family after
the shop closed?
- Paragraph 1 (Rin finds the photograph; no explanation yet)
- Paragraph 2 (the fire, and how the family had to find new work and
  move) ✅
- Paragraph 3 (Rin's reflection on what the photograph means to her)

**Task 3 — Vocabulary in Context**
*Question*: In "it was a small piece of her family's legacy," what
does "legacy" mean?
- A. A type of old photograph.
- B. Something from the past that is passed down and still matters
  today. ✅
- C. A store that sells family photos.

*Design note*: Task 3 reinforces "legacy" — first introduced in Theme
6 at the same Foundation level — through a second, different
narrative context (a family photograph rather than a grandmother's
spoken story), deepening understanding of the same word rather than
simply re-recognizing it.

## 5. Writing Task

**Prompt**: "Rin learned something important by looking at an old
family photograph. Is there an old photo, object, or story in your
family that is special to you? What is it, or what would you like to
know about it?"

**Length**: 2–3 sentences (minimum 2).

**Optional sentence starters**:
- "One old photo in my family..."
- "I would like to know..."
- "This is important because..."

**Useful Vocabulary for This Theme**: history, legacy, event, era,
cause, effect, ancestor. These are the words students have learned
this theme. Use whichever ones naturally help you express your ideas
— you are not expected to use every word. The goal is natural
communication, not vocabulary coverage.

**Why this fits Foundation**: the task asks students to describe a
family memory, object, or question — not to analyze or debate history,
keeping the cognitive demand at the Foundation "Answer" level. Students
who cannot think of a family object can still answer by describing
what they would like to ask or learn.

**Connection to the reading**: mirrors Rin's own experience —
discovering something from the past, becoming curious, and connecting
family history with the present — without asking students to copy her
story.

**Connection to AI Talk**: previews an opener — "Rin became interested
in her family's history after finding an old photograph. Is there
something from your family's past that you would like to know more
about?"
